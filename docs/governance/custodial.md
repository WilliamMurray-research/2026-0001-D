# **Custodial Governance Charter**  
### Unified Operator Architecture Programme  
### Version 1.0 — 14 August 2026  
### Classification: D  

---

## **1. Purpose and Mandate**

This charter establishes the custodial governance model for all repositories, artefacts, documents, and computational systems within the Unified Operator Architecture (UOA) research programme. Its purpose is to preserve structural integrity, enforce standards compliance, maintain provenance, and ensure long‑arc coherence across all domains of the programme.

The charter applies to:

- the **Project Template Framework (UPTF)**  
- the **Unified Asset Registry**  
- the **Knowledge Layer**  
- the **Foundations corpus**  
- all mathematical, computational, and doctrinal artefacts  
- all downstream projects instantiated from the UPTF  

This charter is binding across the entire ecosystem.

---

## **2. Custodial Stewardship Model**

### **2.1 Definition**

The programme operates under a **custodial stewardship model**, meaning:

- the corpus is curated, not crowdsourced  
- the intellectual direction is protected, not democratised  
- the architecture is governed, not influenced by external popularity  
- the programme prioritises correctness, coherence, and reproducibility over visibility  

### **2.2 Custodian Responsibilities**

The custodian is responsible for:

- maintaining structural invariants  
- enforcing standards compliance  
- preserving provenance  
- safeguarding conceptual coherence  
- ensuring long‑arc stability  
- managing classification boundaries  
- approving all modifications to governed artefacts  

The custodian does **not** optimise for external metrics, social feedback, or popularity signals.

---

## **3. Standards Compliance Requirement**

### **3.1 Mandatory Adherence**

All work must strictly adhere to the **Standards Framework**  
**→ 2026‑0001‑D Standards Framework [(github.com in Bing)](https://www.bing.com/search?q="https%3A%2F%2Fgithub.com%2FWilliamMurray-research%2F2026-0001-D")**  
including:

- directory schema  
- naming conventions  
- classification rules  
- versioning workflow  
- registry integration  
- governance surfaces  
- public‑export templates  
- knowledge‑graph integration rules  

Compliance is **not optional**.  
Non‑compliant artefacts are rejected.

### **3.2 Structural Invariants**

The following invariants must be preserved:

- deterministic directory structure  
- identifier immutability  
- classification integrity  
- version‑chain continuity  
- registry completeness  
- provenance traceability  
- semantic coherence within the Knowledge Layer  

These invariants form the backbone of programme‑level governance.

---

## **4. Provenance and Auditability**

### **4.1 Unified Asset Registry**

All artefacts must be:

- catalogued  
- classified  
- versioned  
- cross‑referenced  
- provenance‑tracked  

in the **Unified Asset Registry**.

### **4.2 Immutable Record**

Registry entries are immutable except through governed version increments.  
Historical states must remain accessible for audit and verification.

### **4.3 Corpus‑Wide Traceability**

Every artefact must be traceable through:

- its identifier  
- its classification  
- its version lineage  
- its registry entry  
- its semantic relationships in the Knowledge Layer  

This ensures due‑diligence‑grade auditability.

---

## **5. Governance Surfaces**

### **5.1 Architectural Governance**

The following surfaces are governed:

- UPTF structure  
- registry schema  
- standards documents  
- mathematical curriculum  
- doctrinal Foundations corpus  
- semantic Knowledge Layer  
- computational prototypes  
- operator‑tier training materials  

Changes to these surfaces require custodial approval.

### **5.2 Non‑Governed Surfaces**

Exploratory notebooks, ideation sketches, and non‑canonical prototypes may exist outside governance surfaces but must not violate structural invariants.

---

## **6. Decoupling from External Social Metrics**

### **6.1 Principle**

External social metrics — including stars, forks, followers, trending status, or public engagement — are **explicitly decoupled** from internal architectural governance.

### **6.2 Rationale**

This decoupling ensures:

- epistemic independence  
- structural discipline  
- protection from popularity‑driven distortion  
- insulation from external incentives  
- preservation of long‑arc coherence  

The programme is governed by **internal standards**, not external sentiment.

---

## **7. Confidentiality and Boundary Management**

### **7.1 Private Theoretical Corpus**

The **Unified Operator Architecture (UOA)** remains private until:

- definitions stabilise  
- proofs settle  
- formalisation is underway  
- operator‑tier mathematics is complete  
- governance conditions permit disclosure  

### **7.2 Public Scaffolding**

The following remain public:

- standards  
- registry  
- UPTF  
- Foundations  
- curriculum  
- roadmap  
- computational ecosystem  

This separation preserves intellectual integrity while enabling due‑diligence evaluation.

---

## **8. Amendment Process**

### **8.1 Controlled Evolution**

This charter may be amended only through:

- custodial review  
- standards‑framework update  
- registry‑record modification  
- version‑increment publication  

### **8.2 Versioning**

All amendments must:

- increment the charter version  
- be recorded in the registry  
- be linked to a standards‑framework update  
- preserve structural invariants  

---

## **9. Enforcement**

### **9.1 Authority**

The custodian has full authority to:

- reject non‑compliant artefacts  
- revert violations  
- enforce classification boundaries  
- maintain structural integrity  
- protect the programme’s conceptual direction  

### **9.2 Non‑Compliance**

Non‑compliant contributions:

- are not merged  
- are not archived  
- do not enter the registry  
- do not enter the Knowledge Layer  
- do not become part of the corpus  

---

## **10. Closing Statement**

This charter formalises the governance philosophy underpinning the Unified Operator Architecture programme. It ensures that the corpus remains:

- governed  
- reproducible  
- structurally coherent  
- epistemically disciplined  
- architecturally stable  
- audit‑ready  
- insulated from external distortion  

The custodial model preserves the integrity of the work and protects the long‑arc trajectory of the programme.
